Sightseeing

Museums, monuments and the best views, grouped by neighbourhood so you can tick off a few in one go. The pill on each card is the nearest metro or train stop.

Belém & the riverside 6

Belém is the perfect half-day out: tram 15E or the Cascais train from Cais do Sodré will get you there. Combine Jerónimos, Torre de Belém and MAAT in one go.

Mosteiro dos Jerónimos

♥ Luisa’s fav

My favourite historical site in Lisbon, no contest. Wait until you see the cloister: it's like someone made lace out of stone.

TipBook a time slot and go early, the queue gets long.

Praça do Império, 1400-206 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Torre de Belém

The iconic little tower on the river. It's close to the Padrão dos Descobrimentos, so on a sunny day it's a lovely walk along the water.

TipSame as Jerónimos: you need a time slot.

Av. Brasília, 1400-038 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Padrão dos Descobrimentos

The big stone ship on the riverbank, with Henry the Navigator at the prow and all the explorers lined up behind him. Take the lift to the top: the view over the Torre, Jerónimos and the river is the best in Belém.

TipLook down at the giant compass rose in the pavement in front of it, a gift from South Africa.

Av. Brasília, 1400-038 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

MAAT – Museum of Art, Architecture & Technology

A fresh perspective on art inside a gorgeous wavy building by the river. Great views and a good coffee shop too.

TipWalk up onto the roof for the view, even if you skip the exhibitions.

Av. Brasília, 1300-598 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

MACAM – Museu de Arte Contemporânea Armando Martins

Contemporary art in a beautifully restored palace. A newer spot that's still a bit of a secret.

R. da Junqueira 66, 1300-343 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Palácio da Ajuda

A royal palace up on the hill above Belém. It's also home to the Royal Treasure Museum, which means actual crown jewels.

Largo da Ajuda, 1349-021 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Historic centre: Chiado, Baixa & Alfama 6

Lisbon has seven hills and every one of them is paved with shiny cobblestones (calçada). They're pretty but slippery, so wear comfy shoes!

Museu de São Roque

♥ Luisa’s fav

In Chiado, which is my favourite place in Lisbon. The church next door looks plain from the outside, but inside it's jaw-droppingly golden.

TipEasiest way up: metro to Baixa-Chiado and walk 5 minutes. The Glória funicular is out of service until 2029.

Largo Trindade Coelho, 1200-470 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Castelo de São Jorge

Come here for that Instagram pic. It has the best views over the rooftops and the river.

TipIt's at the very top of a hill. Pair it with the Sé on the way up.

R. de Santa Cruz do Castelo, 1100-129 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Sé de Lisboa

Lisbon's cathedral and the oldest church in the city. It's a solid, fortress-like building with centuries of stories inside.

Largo da Sé 1, 1100-585 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Convento do Carmo

The roofless Gothic ruins of the Carmo convent, left exactly as the 1755 earthquake left them, with a small archaeology museum inside. The famous Santa Justa iron lift next door is closed for works at the moment, but the convent is the real reason to come anyway.

TipWalk up through Chiado to Largo do Carmo, one of the prettiest squares in town. Carmo Rooftop (Drink tab) is right here.

Largo do Carmo, 1200-092 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Praça do Comércio & Arco da Rua Augusta

The grand yellow square that opens onto the river, where the royal palace stood before the earthquake. Climb the arch for the view straight down Rua Augusta to the castle.

TipSit on the steps by the water (Cais das Colunas) at the end of the day.

R. Augusta 2, 1100-053 Lisboa ↗

Alfama & tram 28

The oldest neighbourhood: a maze of alleys, washing on the lines, fado drifting out of windows. The yellow tram 28 rattles right through it on its way up to the castle and Graça.

TipCatch the 28 early in the morning at Martim Moniz to get a seat; by 11h it's a sardine tin.

Largo das Portas do Sol, 1100-411 Lisboa ↗

Off the beaten track 2

LX Factory

An old industrial complex under the 25 de Abril bridge turned into shops, street art, cafés and the Ler Devagar bookshop with its flying bicycle. Best on a Sunday, when there's a market.

TipCombine with Belém: it's two train stops before it.

R. Rodrigues de Faria 103, 1300-501 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Oceanário de Lisboa

One of the best aquariums in the world, built for Expo '98: a huge central tank with sharks, rays and a sunfish, and four ocean habitats around it. Worth it even if you think you don't like aquariums.

TipTake the cable car along the river afterwards, and dinner in Parque das Nações is in the Eat tab.

Esplanada Dom Carlos I, 1990-005 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Museu Calouste Gulbenkian

An amazing art collection plus one of the prettiest gardens in town. It's a short walk from São Sebastião, so you can escape there during a conference break.

TipThe garden is free, so bring a pastry and find a bench.

Av. de Berna 45A, 1067-001 Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Where to eat in Lisbon

My restaurants in the city. Sintra and the coast have their own lists in their tabs.

Breakfast & snacks 1

Portuguese breakfast is a coffee and something from the bakery counter, standing up or not. Savoury is totally normal in the morning.

A Padaria Portuguesa

The bakery chain that's on every other corner (green sign, wooden counters). This is where I'd send you for breakfast, a mid-afternoon snack, or a cheap lunch. What to order: an empada de galinha or de leitão (a little chicken or suckling-pig pie), rissóis de camarão or de carne (crescent-shaped shrimp or meat croquettes), pastéis de bacalhau (salt-cod fritters), a pastel de nata of course, and a pão de Deus (sweet coconut bun) if you want something soft. With a galão (milky coffee in a glass) or a meia de leite.

TipThat one is by Saldanha; there's another on Av. da República 39B and about sixty more around town. Ask for things 'quentinho' (warmed up).

Av. Duque de Ávila 24, Lisboa ↗Website ↗

O Tacho

♥ Luisa’s fav

Dote

♥ Luisa’s fav

Francesinha time. This is Porto's famous sandwich: steak, sausage, ham and cheese, drowned in a spicy beer sauce. Dote makes one of the best in Lisbon, and has a few branches around town.

TipThere are also Dotes in Alvalade (Av. da Igreja 24) and Parque das Nações (Av. Dom João II 43E).

Av. da República 51C, Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Around the city 4

A short list on purpose: the classics I'd send a friend to, in different corners of town.

Cervejaria Ramiro

The most famous seafood house in Lisbon: clams, prawns, crab, and a steak sandwich (prego) for dessert, as tradition demands. Loud, fun, worth the queue.

TipNo reservations. Go early or expect to wait.

Av. Almirante Reis 1H, Lisboa ↗

Taberna da Rua das Flores

A small tavern in Chiado with a menu written on a chalkboard that changes every day. Creative Portuguese petiscos, always full.

TipNo reservations; put your name on the list and have a drink nearby.

Rua das Flores 103, Lisboa ↗

Time Out Market

The big food hall in the old Ribeira market: dozens of Lisbon's best chefs and restaurants under one roof. Perfect when a group can't agree on what to eat.

TipOpen until midnight.

Av. 24 de Julho 49, Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Ponto Final

Across the river in Cacilhas: a 10-minute ferry from Cais do Sodré, then a walk along the water to tables on the quay with the whole of Lisbon in front of you. The best sunset dinner there is.

TipBook, and ask for a table outside.

Rua do Ginjal 72, Cacilhas, Almada ↗

Vegan 5

Lisbon is a surprisingly good city for vegans. All fully plant-based, except Honest Greens, which is veggie-friendly with plenty of vegan dishes.

A Minha Avó

'My grandmother': vegan versions of Portuguese home cooking, and it's right next to the conference. Lunch menus at a great price.

TipLunch only on most days.

Av. António Augusto de Aguiar 74B, Lisboa ↗

Ao 26 Vegan Food Project

The best-known vegan restaurant in Lisbon, in Chiado. Creative, filling and clever with Portuguese flavours.

TipBook for dinner.

Rua da Horta Seca 5, Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Jardim das Cerejas

All-you-can-eat vegan buffet in Chiado, cheap and cheerful. Good for a quick lunch between sights.

Calçada do Sacramento 36, Lisboa ↗

The Food Temple

Hidden in a tiny square in Mouraria: a short, changing menu cooked with love. Dinner only, and the steps outside fill up on warm evenings.

TipBook, it's small.

Beco do Jasmim 18, Lisboa ↗

Honest Greens

♥ Luisa’s fav

Fresh, healthy, fast: build-your-own bowls, roasted veg and good coffee. A chain with lots of vegan options, and the biggest one is a few minutes from the conference.

What to eat before you leave 6

Not places, just dishes: scan any menu for these and order them when you see them.

  • Bacalhau à BrásSalt cod shredded with onions, thin fried potatoes and egg. The friendliest of the 365 cod recipes, and the one to start with.
  • BifanaA pork steak simmered in garlic and white wine, in a soft bread roll. Lisbon's street sandwich: cheap, fast, eaten standing at the counter with a beer.
  • Peixinhos da horta'Little fish from the garden': green beans in a light batter, fried until crisp. No fish involved. Portuguese monks took this to Japan in the 1500s and it became tempura. A great starter, and vegan.
  • Arroz de mariscoA soupy, saffron-tinted rice full of prawns, clams and crab, served in a pot for two. The dish to share by the sea.
  • Polvo à lagareiroOctopus roasted until the tips crisp, drowned in olive oil and garlic, with smashed potatoes. Better than it sounds, and it sounds great.
  • Pastel de nata vs pastel de BelémSame custard tart, two names: pastéis de Belém are only the ones from the 1837 bakery in Belém, everything else is a pastel de nata. Try both warm, with cinnamon, and pick a side.

Where to drink in Lisbon

Sunsets, rooftops and Pink Street. Start high, end low.

Sunset at a miradouro 3

Lisbon's viewpoints come with little kiosks (quiosques) selling beer, wine and ginjinha. Grab a drink, sit on the wall, watch the light go orange. Cheapest and best bar in town.

Miradouro de Santa Catarina

♥ Luisa’s fav

The 'Adamastor', named after the stone sea monster in the middle. Young, scruffy, guitars and sunsets over the river. Buy a beer at the kiosk and join everyone on the wall.

TipWalk down to Cais do Sodré afterwards for the rest of the night (see below).

Rua de Santa Catarina, Lisboa ↗

Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara

The postcard view of the castle and the old town, with a very civilised kiosk under the trees. The Glória funicular that used to climb here is out of service until 2029, so walk up Calçada da Glória from Restauradores (steep, 8 minutes) or come from Baixa-Chiado.

Rua Dom Pedro V 4, Lisboa ↗

Miradouro da Graça

Across the valley on the other hill, next to a convent: pine trees, a kiosk and the whole city in front of you. Quieter, more local. Tram 28 stops just below, if you don't fancy the hill.

Calçada da Graça, Lisboa ↗

Rooftops 6

Pricier than a kiosk, but the views are worth one evening. Most open around 16h–17h and the good ones fill up at sunset: go early or book.

Carmo Rooftop

On the Terraços do Carmo, right next to the ruined Convento do Carmo and the Santa Justa lift. Cocktails, DJs, and outdoor cinema and jazz nights in summer. The Chiado rooftop of the moment.

Largo do Carmo, Lisboa ↗

Bar Entretanto

7th floor of the Hotel do Chiado: a smaller, calmer terrace with the castle straight ahead and the river below. Good for a quiet first drink before dinner.

Rua Nova do Almada 114, Lisboa ↗

Beyond Rooftop Bar

The newest one (opened in 2025) on top of the Lisbon Chiado Hotel, with a full 360° view. Cocktails and small plates.

Largo Rafael Bordalo Pinheiro 20, Lisboa ↗

Park

The original cool rooftop: a bar hidden on top of a multi-storey car park. Take the lift to the 6th floor and walk up one more. Plants, DJs, sunset over the 25 de Abril bridge.

TipThe entrance really is just a car park door. Trust it.

Calçada do Combro 58, Lisboa ↗

TOPO Martim Moniz

6th floor of a slightly shabby shopping centre, which is exactly the charm: the castle is right in front of you, lit up at night.

Praça Martim Moniz, 6th floor, Lisboa ↗

Attiko

12th floor of the ME Lisbon hotel, 3 minutes from the conference: Mediterranean-Asian cocktails and a panoramic view of the park. The closest rooftop to São Sebastião.

Av. António Augusto de Aguiar 2, Lisboa ↗

Cais do Sodré 5

Once the sailors' red-light district, now where Lisbon goes out. Start on the terraces, end on Pink Street (Rua Nova do Carvalho) after midnight.

Javá

Rooftop on top of the old post office building, reached by a panoramic lift. Cocktails, DJs and a view over the river and the ferries. The best sunset in Cais do Sodré.

Praça Dom Luís I 30, Lisboa ↗

Pensão Amor

♥ Luisa’s fav

A former brothel turned into a velvet-and-mirrors bar on Pink Street: burlesque décor, cocktails, a tiny bookshop and occasional shows. Wonderfully weird.

Rua Nova do Carvalho 38, Lisboa ↗

Sol e Pesca

An old fishing-tackle shop turned bar: the walls are still full of rods and hooks. Order a beer and a tin of Portuguese sardines or mackerel with bread. Very Lisbon.

Rua Nova do Carvalho 44, Lisboa ↗

O Bom O Mau e O Vilão

'The Good, the Bad and the Ugly': old sofas, live music and DJs most nights, no dress code. Where the night actually starts for a lot of people.

Rua do Alecrim 21, Lisboa ↗

A Tabacaria

A tiny 1900s tobacconist's turned craft-cocktail bar, with the original wooden counter. Only a few seats, so go early or be patient.

Rua de São Paulo 75, Lisboa ↗

Clubs 6

Lisbon goes out late: nobody arrives at a club before 1am, and the good hours are 2 to 5. Most charge a small entry that includes a drink.

Lux Frágil

The Lisbon club, since 1998: three floors in an old warehouse by the river, with techno and house downstairs, a bar with sofas upstairs and a terrace over the water. If you only do one club, do this one.

TipDoor policy exists. Check who's playing and don't turn up in a big loud group.

Av. Infante D. Henrique, Armazém A, Cais da Pedra, Lisboa ↗Website ↗

Ministerium

Serious techno inside a grand old ministry building on Praça do Comércio, with a terrace that looks over the square. Club nights are mostly weekends; check the programme.

Praça do Comércio 72, Lisboa ↗

B.Leza

Live African music, kizomba and funaná in a big hall on the Cais do Gás, by the river. Unlike anywhere else in town: everyone dances, and the band is always good.

TipOpens around 22h–23h; the live set is usually midnight-ish, then DJs.

Cais do Gás 1, Lisboa ↗

Jamaica

A Lisbon institution since 1971 that moved from Pink Street to the Cais do Gás: 80s and 90s classics, rock and pop, everybody singing. Zero pretension, maximum fun.

TipRight next to B.Leza, so you can hop between the two.

Cais do Gás 1, Lisboa ↗

Titanic Sur Mer

A concert bar on the quay in Cais do Sodré: a terrace on the river by day, live bands and DJs at night, very mixed and very local.

Cais do Gás, Rua da Cintura do Porto de Lisboa, Lisboa ↗

Incógnito

The indie and alternative basement club, thirty years and counting, hidden behind an unmarked door near São Bento. Small, sweaty, great music.

TipRing the bell. Really.

Rua dos Poiais de São Bento 37, Lisboa ↗

Sintra

Palaces in the forest, a castle on the ridge and the best pastries outside Lisbon. Plan a full day.

🚆 Train from Rossio · about 40 min

Things to see

In the old town 2

Go early on a weekday: Sintra gets packed, especially at weekends. From the station, a hop-on bus goes up the hill to Pena and the Moorish Castle, because the walk up is long and steep.

Palácio Nacional de Sintra

The palace right in the middle of town, the one with the two giant white chimneys. Inside, look up: the painted ceilings are the best part.

Largo Rainha Dona Amélia, Sintra ↗Website ↗

Quinta da Regaleira

Mysterious gardens full of grottoes, tunnels and towers. Walk down the Initiation Well, a spiral staircase that goes deep into the ground. Everyone's favourite.

TipBring a phone torch for the tunnels.

Rua Barbosa du Bocage 5, Sintra ↗Website ↗

Up the hill 3

Palácio da Pena

The colourful fairytale palace on top of the hill, the postcard of Sintra. Yellow and red walls, and views all the way to the sea.

TipBuy a timed ticket online before you go.

Estrada da Pena, Sintra ↗Website ↗

Castelo dos Mouros

Old Moorish walls zig-zagging along the ridge. The view over Sintra and the ocean is amazing, and it's right next to Pena, so do both together.

Serra de Sintra, next to Pena ↗Website ↗

Palácio de Monserrate

A romantic palace with a huge botanical garden. Much quieter than Pena, perfect if you want a calmer afternoon.

Estrada de Monserrate, Sintra ↗Website ↗

Further out 1

Cabo da Roca

The westernmost point of mainland Europe: cliffs, a lighthouse and the Atlantic. Great at sunset, and very windy, so bring a jacket.

TipTake the bus from Sintra or Cascais station.

Cabo da Roca, Colares ↗

Where to eat

Travesseiros & queijadas 4

Sintra's two famous sweets: the travesseiro (a warm puff-pastry 'pillow' filled with almond cream) and the queijada (a little cheese tart). You need both.

Casa do Preto

♥ Luisa’s favest. 1931

My favourite. One of the oldest queijada bakeries in Sintra, still family-run and much calmer than the centre. Locals come here.

TipIt's on the way in from Portela de Sintra station, so stop here first.

Estrada Chão de Meninos 40, Sintra ↗

Piriquita

est. 1862

The original home of the travesseiro, right in the old town. Expect a queue; it moves fast and the travesseiros come out warm.

Rua das Padarias 1, Sintra ↗

Ginja Mariquinhas

The ginjinha stop of the old town, right next to Piriquita II: a shot of sour-cherry liqueur served in a little chocolate cup, which you then eat. Sweet, strong and very Portuguese. Opened in 2026, but the ginja is an old Óbidos recipe.

TipOrder it 'com elas' (with the cherries in). Chocolate cup included.

Rua das Padarias 16, Sintra ↗

Piriquita II

The second shop, a few steps up the same street. Same pastries, usually a shorter queue, and tables with a view.

Rua das Padarias 18, Sintra ↗

Tacho Real

A Sintra classic, up a little hill: proper Portuguese cooking, seafood and a terrace. The place for a longer, sit-down lunch.

Rua da Ferraria 4, Sintra ↗

Volta e Meia

Relaxed restaurant-bar on the Volta do Duche, the pretty road between the station and the old town. Good food, good cocktails, no fuss.

Volta do Duche 2, Sintra ↗

Nau Palatina

Small, cosy and very well loved: homemade petiscos and a great list of Portuguese wines. Dinner only.

TipTiny, so book a table.

Calçada de São Pedro 18, Sintra ↗Website ↗

A minuscule, cheap and cheerful tasca inside the local philharmonic society. Petiscos, wine and sometimes live music. Pure Sintra.

Oeiras & Cascais

The coast west of Lisbon: beaches, a seaside promenade and a pretty old fishing town. The train runs right along the water.

🚆 Train from Cais do Sodré · about 40 min to Cascais

Things to see

Oeiras & Carcavelos 2

Everything here is on the Cascais train from Cais do Sodré, the same line that stops in Belém.

Praia de Santo Amaro & the Oeiras promenade

Oeiras's own beach, with a seaside promenade that runs along the water to Paço de Arcos. A gentle walk, a swim in summer, and a kiosk for a beer at the end.

TipThe Marquês de Pombal palace gardens up in town are closed for works at the moment, so skip those.

Praia de Santo Amaro de Oeiras, Oeiras ↗

Praia de Carcavelos

The big beach of the Lisbon coast: long sand, surf schools and a fort at one end. In summer it's where all of Lisbon goes.

Praia de Carcavelos, Carcavelos ↗

Estoril & Cascais 5

The Paredão

The seafront promenade from São João do Estoril to Cascais, about 3 km along the water. Best at the end of the afternoon, with a stop at a beach bar.

TipWalk it one way and take the train back.

Passeio Marítimo, Estoril to Cascais ↗

Cascais old town & Cidadela

Cobbled streets, the old fishermen's bay, the little Praia da Rainha and the Cidadela, a fortress that now has a hotel, shops and art inside.

Cascais centre ↗

Casa das Histórias Paula Rego

A museum for the painter Paula Rego, in a striking red building with two pyramid towers. Her work is dark, funny and unforgettable.

Avenida da República 300, Cascais ↗

Boca do Inferno

'The Mouth of Hell': a cliff arch where the waves crash in. Most dramatic on a rough day.

Avenida Rei Humberto II, Cascais ↗

Santini

The most famous ice cream on the coast. A Cascais institution, and the queue is part of the experience.

Av. Valbom 28F, Cascais ↗

Further out 1

Praia do Guincho

A wild beach with big dunes and lots of wind, loved by kitesurfers. Not for swimming on rough days, but perfect for the sunset.

TipTake the bus from Cascais station.

Praia do Guincho, Cascais ↗

Where to eat

Oeiras & Paço de Arcos 5

Paço de Arcos is one stop after Oeiras on the train. The old village by the beach is small, so everything is a short walk from the station.

Getting around

From the airport to the conference in three steps, then the whole network in one map.

Step 1Get a transport card

The metro station is inside the airport. Buy a card at the machines and top it up. It works on metro, trains, trams and buses.

Step 2Take the red line

Direction São Sebastião. No changes needed.

Step 3Get off at the last stop

São Sebastião is the end of the line, so you can’t miss it. That’s our stop!
